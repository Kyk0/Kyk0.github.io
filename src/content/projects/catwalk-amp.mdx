## Summary

Motion tracking reproduces one clip. Adversarial motion priors instead learn a *style*:
a discriminator scores how much the robot's motion looks like a set of reference clips,
and that score is added to the velocity-tracking reward. Here the references are two
runway walks retargeted to the K1, so the robot walks wherever it is commanded, in a
catwalk style.

## Method

- Two 9.5 s catwalk clips generated from text with NVIDIA's [Kimodo](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/sil/projects/kimodo/)
  motion diffusion model (for example: *"A female fashion model walks quickly forward with very
  short, quick crossover steps and subtle hip sway…"*), then retargeted to the K1 with GMR.
- Velocity task with AMP in mjlab (`booster_mjlab`), PPO with the Muon optimizer,
  4096 environments.
- The stock setup shows the discriminator only the legs. I added the shoulders and elbows
  (46 features per frame instead of 30), and made the style weight, the upper-body posture
  penalty and the discriminator width configurable.

## Results

With the discriminator on the legs only, the shoulders already swing with the gait, but
the elbows stay almost frozen: about 15–18% of the reference elbow swing. Adding the arms to
the discriminator, relaxing the upper-body posture penalty from −0.1 to −0.01 and raising
the style weight to 0.7 brings the elbow swing to **0.91–1.10 of the reference**, with the
left–right arm phase correlation at +0.70 (reference +0.71). The −0.01 weight is the one
that matches the arm motion measured on the reference clips; with no penalty at all, the
arms swing wider but drift out of phase. The cost is about 20% worse velocity tracking.

I re-ran 12 trained catwalk policies under the same command (0.5 m/s forward, 12 s);
none of them fell.
