## Summary

**Co-author.** This is joint work with Raiyan Ashraf (lead), Yanni Liu, Sruthi Ganji,
Jong-Hoon Kim and Ju Yup Lee.

LLM-based social robots mostly react to the last thing said. Mi-RoL gives the
conversation a structure based on Motivational Interviewing (MI), and grounds it in the
user's situation: what the robot sees, what it knows about the user, and what they
talked about before.

## How it works

The framework splits **reasoning** from **response**. At each stage boundary a bigger
reasoning model looks at the context, estimates engagement and emotion, picks the
strategy and decides when to move on. A fast response model says the actual lines in
between. The stages are Intro, Engage, Affirm & Reflect, Evoke and Expand.

Speech is transcribed with Whisper and Silero voice activity detection. GPT-4o picks out
the user's appearance and the objects around them, and that goes into one shared
context together with the user's personal info.

<Figure src="/media/mi-rol/system-overview.png" alt="Mi-RoL system overview: personal information, visual cues and speech feed a unified context; a reasoning layer and a response layer drive five MI stages" caption="System overview: the reasoning layer plans and moves between MI stages, the response layer talks." />

Nine model pairings were compared first: three reasoning models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro,
Llama 3 8B) against three response models (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama 3.2 3B),
rated by four researchers. GPT-4o for reasoning with Gemini 2.5 Flash for responses came
out on top and was used in the study.

## Study

40 participants, between-subject, with a physical Pepper. Each person had a 7–10 minute
conversation with either Mi-RoL or a baseline: unstructured, reactive dialogue from a
single GPT-4o.

| | Baseline | Mi-RoL | effect size d |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Perceived empathy | 3.46 | 3.90 | 0.68 |
| User engagement | 3.58 | 4.06 | 0.75 |
| Social presence | 3.15 | 3.88 | 0.88 |
| Intention to use | 3.55 | 4.12 | 0.65 |

All four were significantly better with Mi-RoL (5-point scales, p < .05), and social
presence improved the most. People liked it most when the robot noticed something they
had with them, like a backpack or a tumbler, and connected it to their own stories.
